## Overview

Every interaction at the Edmonton Office lot that involves a decision, a request, or a compliance issue has a defined communication protocol. This file documents those protocols with exact language where source material provides it.

**Core principle:** "Inspect what you expect." Standards only hold through daily, consistent enforcement — not one-time announcements. Every enforcement conversation follows a defined protocol, not improvised judgment.

---

## 1. Before Moving Any Vehicle — Pre-Move Check

Before any vehicle is moved for any reason, the lot team must verify its status.

### Protocol
1. Identify the assigned salesperson for the vehicle
2. Ask: **"Whose is this? What's the status? When is delivery?"**
3. Based on the answer, follow the appropriate branch below

### Branch A: Salesperson says delivery is imminent
- **Action:** Expedite the vehicle through PDI and detail
- **Do NOT skip PDI or detail** — the customer waits if necessary; the process does not get skipped
- Contact the service department immediately: provide stock number, VIN, and delivery date
- Request priority PDI processing

### Branch B: Salesperson says "don't worry about it, it's good to go" or attempts to skip PDI
- **Response (exact language):**
  > "Stellantis requires final PDI within 2 days. We're at 80% compliance, in the red zone. It needs to go through."
- This statement is not negotiable. PDI happens. Period.
- If salesperson continues to push back: escalate to Sales Manager (Kevin) and report the situation

### Branch C: Salesperson confirms the vehicle is not ready for delivery
- **Action:** Process the vehicle normally through PDI → detail → placement
- No urgency escalation needed; standard workflow applies

## 2. Documentation — Team Chats

All task assignments, issues identified, and requests made during and after the morning lot walk must be documented in team chats.

### Why Documentation Matters
- Creates an accountability trail
- If an issue recurs, there is a record of when it was first identified and what action was requested
- Allows management to track response times and compliance rates
- Prevents "I didn't know" responses to known issues

### What to Document
- Every issue identified during the morning lot walk
- Every task assigned (vehicle routed to service, sign requested, vehicle repositioned)
- Every escalation initiated
- Every response received to an escalation

## 3. Sold Sign Enforcement — 3-Strike Escalation Protocol

When a vehicle is identified as sold but does not have a sold sign with the customer name, the lot manager escalates using this exact 3-strike protocol directed at the Sales Manager (Kevin).

### Strike 1 — Polite, helpful request

**Initiated when:** Sold sign is missing and a reasonable amount of time has passed
**Channel:** Direct conversation (in-person or phone) or team chat
**Language:**
> "Hey, Kevin, do you mind getting your guys to put a sold sign in there?"

**Expected response:** Sales team places the sign.

### Strike 2 — Reminder with slightly more emphasis

**Initiated when:** Reasonable time has passed since Strike 1 with no action
**Channel:** Direct conversation (in-person or phone) or team chat
**Language:**
> "Hey, Kevin, do you mind?"

*(Same request, but tone conveys that this is a second ask — not a first)*

**Expected response:** Sales team places the sign.

### Strike 3 — Direct accountability conversation

**Initiated when:** Strike 2 has produced no result; the missing sign is now a pattern or a persistent issue
**Channel:** Direct, in-person conversation preferred
**Language:**
> "Kev, killing me, buddy. Can you just tell me the stuff and I'll do it, because we have to be organized."

**Expected result:** Self-correction. Sales Manager takes personal ownership and places the sign himself.
**Confirmed response pattern:** "No, no, buddy, I'm coming down myself. I'll do it myself."

## 4. Staff Parking Violation Protocol

### First Violation — New Employee

**Context:** Employee is new and was not informed of the parking rule before starting.

**Approach:** Welcoming and respectful. Not confrontational. Not critical of the employee as a person.

**Language:**
> "Hey, welcome aboard. All sales have to park on the street — limited space, landlord parking, big trucks coming through."

**Framing:** This is standard practice. It's the way it works here. It is not personal.

**Referenced example:** Chris's son (a new employee trying to get into the industry) was used as the example in the walkthrough. Nobody told him the rules. The correct response is to inform him kindly — not to confront him.

**Expected outcome:** Employee understands and complies going forward.

## 7. Communication Standard

All communication involving compliance, task routing, or escalation must occur through a channel that creates a documentation trail. Direct (in-person) conversations must be followed up with a team chat message summarizing what was communicated and what action was agreed upon.

**Why:** If an issue recurs after being communicated, the documentation proves the issue was raised and allows management to hold the appropriate party accountable.
