## Overview

Every vehicle on the Edmonton Office lot must have correct identification signage at all times. The absence of signage creates operational paralysis — staff cannot determine a vehicle's status, cannot move it correctly, and cannot sell or deliver it without risking errors.

**Core principle:** Any person walking the lot should be able to instantly identify the status of every vehicle without asking anyone. The signage system makes this possible.

---

## 1. Stock-In Tags

### Purpose
Confirms that a vehicle has been received, processed, and entered into inventory. The absence of a stock-in tag on any vehicle means it has NOT been formally processed. No vehicle should be on the lot without one.

### Who Creates Them
- Admin — Stock Tags (Jorja) fills out stock-in tags
- Admin/Tech — Stock Tags + Service (Giselle) also fills out stock-in tags
- Both fill tags and hand completed tags to the lot team

### Process (Step-by-Step)
1. Vehicle arrives on lot
2. Admin — Stock Tags (Jorja) or Admin/Tech (Giselle) fills out the stock-in tag with vehicle information
3. Completed tag is handed to the lot team
4. Lot team places the tag on the vehicle — bottom-right corner of windshield, no exceptions
5. Vehicle is now confirmed as processed

### Placement Location
- **Bottom-right corner of the windshield** — always, consistently, on every vehicle, no exceptions

### Color
- **White** — this lot uses white stock-in tags
- Do NOT use yellow tags — yellow tags look like AutoWorld tags and cause confusion

### Current Status at Time of Walkthrough
**This process was NOT active at the time of the walkthrough.** Needs immediate activation. Every vehicle on the lot must be retroactively tagged, and the tag creation / handoff / placement process must be established as a daily routine.

### Rule
Every vehicle on the Edmonton Office lot must have a stock-in tag at all times. No exceptions.
- No tag = vehicle has not been formally processed
- No tag = vehicle should not be in any active zone
- If you find a vehicle without a tag during the morning lot walk: flag it immediately and route for tagging before any other action

## 2. Sold Signs

### Purpose
Identifies vehicles that have been sold. Allows anyone walking the lot to immediately recognize a sold vehicle and avoid moving it, re-selling it, or confusing its status.

### When Placed
**Immediately when a vehicle is sold** — not at the end of the day, not when the paperwork is finalized, not when the customer picks it up. The moment a sale is completed, the sold sign goes in the vehicle.

### Who Places It
- The salesperson who completed the deal places the sign, OR
- The lot team places it when requested via team chat (exact request: "Hey, please go put a sold sign in [vehicle]")

### Content
- Customer name written on the sign with a Sharpie
- Sign is placed visibly inside the vehicle

### Location of Supplies
- Upstairs (in the dealership building)
- Sharpies are kept with the sold signs

### Enforcement Protocol (3-Strike Approach)
When a sold sign is missing after a vehicle has been sold, the lot manager enforces using the 3-strike approach:

| Strike | Action | Exact Language |
|---|---|---|
| Strike 1 | Polite, helpful request | "Hey, Kevin, do you mind getting your guys to put a sold sign in there?" |
| Strike 2 | Reminder with slightly more emphasis | "Hey, Kevin, do you mind?" |
| Strike 3 | Direct accountability conversation | "Kev, killing me, buddy. Can you just tell me the stuff and I'll do it, because we have to be organized." |
| Expected result | Self-correction by sales team | "No, no, buddy, I'm coming down myself. I'll do it myself." |

## 3. Trade-In Banners

### Purpose
Distinguishes trade-in vehicles from active inventory, sold vehicles, and customer vehicles. When a trade-in sits on the lot without a banner, it cannot be identified and creates confusion about its destination.

### When Placed
**Immediately when a trade-in is received from a customer.**

### Who Places It
Lot team places the trade-in banner at the moment the customer's trade-in vehicle arrives.

### Location of Supplies
Upstairs (same location as sold sign supplies).

---

## 4. Customer Vehicle Signs

### Purpose
Identifies a customer's personal vehicle that is on the lot for any reason (waiting during service, dropping off for work, picking up later). These vehicles are NOT dealership inventory.

### Content
**Exact text:** "Customer's car, picking up [date]"
- Include the expected pickup date
- Sign is placed visibly on or in the vehicle

### When Placed
Immediately when a customer's personal vehicle arrives on the lot.

### Importance
Without this sign, a customer's vehicle cannot be distinguished from inventory. Staff may attempt to move it, detail it, or otherwise interfere with a vehicle that belongs to a customer. The sign protects the customer and eliminates confusion.

## 5. Consequence of Missing Signage — The Kia Incident

> **CONFIRMED REAL INCIDENT — This happened at the Edmonton Office lot.**

A Kia sat on the Edmonton Office lot for **6 weeks** with no identification signage of any kind.

During those 6 weeks:
- No staff member could determine whether the Kia was an inventory vehicle, a sold vehicle, a trade-in, or a customer's personal car
- No one moved it, processed it, or made any decision about it
- The vehicle sat completely idle
- No revenue was generated from it
- No appropriate action was taken because no one knew what action was appropriate

**This is the canonical example of why every vehicle must have status signage at all times.**

The absence of a single tag or sign resulted in 6 weeks of total paralysis around one vehicle.
